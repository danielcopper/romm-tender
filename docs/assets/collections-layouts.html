Tender Collections Layouts #1833 · panel 854 px wide, page body 263 px tall on the Deck

PAGE
What is here
Every collection RomM lists to you (your own, and other users' public ones), each with one switch: sync it or not. RomM has three kinds. Standard collections are picked by hand, and the favorites collection RomM keeps for starred games is one of them. Smart collections are saved searches, so their games change as matches come and go. Virtual collections are groupings RomM builds from game metadata; Tender shows two of their types, franchises and IGDB collections. They have no owner.
What turning one on does
It adds a shortcut for every game in the collection, including games on platforms you do not sync. It also groups them in a Steam collection named after it (RomM: [name] (host), with a type label when the setting that keeps same-named collections apart is on). The Collections tab says neither of these today.
What the page knows
RomM's listing gives each collection its name, its kind, its ROM count, which games are in it (their ids), its owner's name (standard and smart only) and whether it is public. Tender passes on the name, the kind (and, for virtual ones, franchise or IGDB collection), the count, whether it is yours, and whether it syncs (plus its id and whether it is the favorites collection), and drops the member ids, the owner's name and whether it is public. With the member ids, how many of a collection's games are already in Steam is a local lookup against the shortcuts Tender made. It is approximate where several versions of one game share a shortcut. None of this costs more than the four requests one page load makes today; how big a collection is on disk would.
Mine / All is not a filter
Under Mine, other users' collections are also left out of the sync, even ones switched on earlier. It is a setting shaped like a filter. Virtual collections have no owner, so it never touches them.
Width and height
Content is 806 px on every display. Height is the scarce axis: after the title, Back and the tab row, the Deck leaves about 263 px. The hatched area on each board is what the Deck does not show without scrolling. The names and numbers are invented.

What it costs

  • The Mine / All switch still looks like a filter while it removes collections from the sync. Placing it next to the search makes that worse.

  • On Virtual, line one holds three segmented controls and a search field. In German (Standard · Intelligent · Virtuell, Meine · Alle, Suchen) it wraps, and the table loses a row.

What it costs

Notes

  1. 1

    Mine / All becomes a toggle that says what it does: Other users' collections, on or off. Off means they are hidden here and left out of the sync, which is what Mine does today. It sits at the head of the column, where Platforms puts its own column-wide controls, because it applies to two of the kinds.

  2. 2

    Favorites becomes a row with its switch in it, like a platform row. The number in the row is today's "Includes 14 favorited ROMs". With no favorites collection, the row stays, greyed, with a dash for the count.

  3. 3

    What turning a collection on does, once per kind, worded for that kind. This is where the page finally says that collections bring in games from platforms you do not sync.

  4. 4

    The table orders collections that are on above those that are off, and freezes that order while the page is open, the same as Platforms. The Owner column appears only on the two kinds that have owners, and names the owner: you, or their RomM user name.

  5. 5

    In Steam says how many of a collection's games already have a shortcut, so the difference to ROMs is roughly what turning it on adds. It is worked out from the member ids the listing carries against Tender's own shortcuts, approximate where versions of one game share a shortcut, and costs no request. Enable all / Disable all ask first whenever no search narrows the list, on every kind, the way today's whole-kind view does.

What it costs

  • Moving between kinds is a walk down a list, not a single press on a segmented control. Search already works one kind at a time today, so nothing is lost there.
  • The virtual kind becomes two rows, Franchises and IGDB collections, under Autogenerated, which follows RomM's own heading. The sub-filter row disappears; the heading costs one line of the column.
  • The owner toggle is a switch outside a selectable row, at the head of a list-and-detail column. Platforms' Enable all / Disable all sit in the same slot, so the frame already supports it.
  • Once the answer arrives, walking the kind rows costs nothing: every count is local, so focus-selects has no reads to hold back.
Recommendation C. Kind is where the user already navigates, so it becomes the list, and the two segmented rows disappear instead of being squeezed. The owner switch stops pretending to be a filter. The promise of what turning a collection on does gets a sentence per kind. It needs no new server request, and it reuses Platforms' list and detail, its column header and the pane table. What a per-collection pane (B) would show — the owner, and how many of a collection's games are already in Steam — fits as two columns of C's table, so the pane buys nothing the table lacks.

Settled after this study

  • C is the shape.
  • The owner switch reads Other users' collections; on means what All means today, and the behaviour is unchanged.
  • The kinds follow RomM's own headings where it has them — RomM's Collections, Smart Collections and Autogenerated collections become Collections, Smart collections and Autogenerated — with Favorites, and Franchises and IGDB collections under Autogenerated. The Steam names the type-distinguishing setting builds come from the same words: a hand-picked collection carries no suffix, the others a short singular — (Smart), (Franchise) and (IGDB Collection), which keeps its capital (qam-panel.md § Library says why) — and a virtual collection of no known type carries (Autogenerated). The keys on the wire and in the settings stay standard / smart / virtual.
  • An In Steam column, from the member ids the listing already carries, checked against Tender's shortcuts; approximate where versions of one game share a shortcut.
  • The owner column names the owner: you, or their RomM user name.
  • Enable all / Disable all ask first whenever no search narrows the list, on every kind.
  • Collections that are on come first, frozen while the page is open, as on Platforms.
  • #1020: a failed read says what went wrong, and leaving the tab and coming back asks again.
