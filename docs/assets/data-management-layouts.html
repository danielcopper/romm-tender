PAGE
What is here
Five destructive or janitorial operations that the narrow panel kept in a "Danger Zone": remove Tender's shortcuts, uninstall downloaded ROM files, delete orphaned grid artwork, remove non-Steam games Tender never added, and clean up games RomM no longer has.
What went wrong in the first three drafts
They all asked how do five operations sit in a menu, and every grouping needed a name for a category that only existed on screen — pick what goes, bulk actions, remove a whole kind. A category nobody can name is not a category. The pages below ask a different question: what does this page SHOW, and what are its rows?
Already settled
The review of the removed-games cleanup stays a dialog (up to 720 × 406 — bigger in both axes than a detail pane's 530 × ~364), redrawn with the options in two columns, a real table, and the run controls pinned at the bottom. That decision is independent of the four shapes below: each of them opens the same dialog.
Navigation
Focus selects in a list, so a scan is always a press and never a selection. B leaves the page. A row must be focusable to be reachable, and a region scrolls only by moving focus.

What the page can say the moment it opens

Three of the four shapes want numbers. This is what exists today, and it is the part that decides how honest a page can be on arrival rather than after a press.

Notes

  1. 1

    Every row starts with a verb, so no heading has to carry one and no category has to be invented. Repetition is a feature on a page that deletes things.

  2. 2

    The count is what the button label used to carry — today's Remove 63 Non-Steam Games becomes a row that says 63, and the full sentence sits on the button in the pane.

What it costs

  • The page shows nothing. It is five doors; you learn what is behind one by walking onto it. What Tender has actually put on this device — how many shortcuts, how much disk, how many leftovers — is never on screen at once, and that is the question a user opens this page with.
  • Two of the five rows say scan rather than a number, and in a menu of verbs that reads as a missing feature rather than as "nobody has looked yet".
  • It is a list of buttons wearing a list-and-detail layout: the pane exists to hold one button and a sentence.

Notes

  1. 1

    No category has to be named, and that is the point. Every row is a population — a thing you have — so the list needs neither headings nor a taxonomy. The failure of three naming attempts was the evidence that the rows were wrong, not the names.

  2. 2

    The page answers a question people actually arrive with — "what has this thing put on my Deck, and how much space is it using?" Deleting is then a consequence of what you see, not a menu you pick from blind.

  3. 3

    Recovery bundles become a row, and the moment they do it is obvious they need one: they are sealed copies that take disk and nothing in the panel lists them today. That the shape reveals a missing feature is a good sign about the shape.

  4. 4

    Rows that need a scan say so in place, and a scan stays a press — Grid images reads "scan" until you ask, and then it reads "118 orphaned of 3 402" forever after.

  5. 5

    "Other non-Steam games" reads right here where it never did in a menu: the list is the things on this device, and these are the ones Tender did not put there. The odd operation becomes an ordinary row.

What it costs

  • Numbers the backend does not have. The ROM-file count is a new callable (cheap); the size on disk is a walk of every install, which is the slow part of the cleanup's preview today. Either the page states counts and not sizes, or the sizes arrive with their own scan.
  • One row is not a possession but a condition — Gone from RomM. It fits (it is a set of things this device holds), but it is the row most likely to want the notice treatment on Main as well.
  • More surface than a menu: six panes to write and test rather than five sentences and five buttons.
  • A row whose count is unknown and whose scan you never press stays a row that shows nothing — the menu's flaw, confined to one line instead of the page.

Notes

  1. 1

    The whole footprint in one screen. No selection, no pane, no walking — the page is an answer rather than a menu, and the footer adds the one number nothing else shows (free space and what the recovery bundles take).

  2. 2

    The action column is the confirmation surface too — two-tap turns Remove all into Confirm, in place, which is how the platform detail's BIOS table already works.

What it costs

  • Every explanation shrinks to one line under the name. Today's sentences — what a removal leaves behind, why the whitelist matters, that RetroDECK can be taken — do not fit, and this is the page where that text is the safety rail.
  • A row with a button is one focus stop, so the row cannot also be a stop that reveals more. The detail has nowhere to go but a dialog, and then three of the five rows open dialogs.
  • It grows badly: a sixth and seventh population (recovery bundles, save files?) push the table under the fold, where the whole argument for this shape was that everything is visible.
  • It is the one shape that no other page in the panel uses — Sync's preview is a table, but it is a table of one kind of thing, not a page built as a table.

D · No page at all

The most radical reading of "maybe the page is wrong": there is no Data Management page. Each operation moves to the page that owns the thing it touches, and the menu entry disappears. Drawn as two sketches, because the point is where things land rather than how they look.

Notes

  1. 1

    Every action next to the thing it acts on. The cleanup is what to do after the server changed, which is what the Sync page is about; the library-wide removals are the "all platforms" version of buttons Library already has.

  2. 2

    One page fewer, and the panel's menu loses an entry whose name never said what was inside it.

What it costs

  • Destructive actions get scattered across three pages, and two of them land on pages people visit for ordinary work. "Where do I go to clean up?" stops having an answer.
  • Two of the five have no home. Orphaned grid images and non-Steam games belong to no page in the panel — they would sit in Settings › Advanced, which is where things go to be lost.
  • It is a much larger change than #1817: Sync, Library, Settings, Main's menu and the register's "one home per action" table all move at once.
  • The register's rule that a notice names a condition and jumps to its home still needs a home to jump to — here the home is a section of another page, which is what Settings sections already do, so the rule survives. That is the one thing this shape does not break.

Open questions, whichever shape wins

  • Sizes or only counts. An inventory is far better with "128 GB" than with "214 files", and that number costs a walk of every install. It could arrive per row on a press, like the scans.
  • Where a run in flight is announced. A cleanup runs for minutes; Main has a conditional slot and notice rows. The same decision in every shape.
  • Recovery bundles. Three shapes above make them visible. Nothing in the panel lists or deletes them today — that is a new capability, and it is out of #1817's scope unless you want it in.
  • Whether non-Steam games belongs to Tender at all. In B and C it reads as an ordinary row ("things on this device Tender did not add"). In A and D it stays the odd one out.
